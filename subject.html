Subject Profile

A summary of what is consistently reported. Where accounts disagree, the disagreement is recorded rather than resolved.

Region
Ashland and the upper Rogue Valley, Oregon

Accounts describe a single hooded figure, usually seen at a distance, usually still, and usually alone. The figure is associated with trails, water and footbridges, and with small practical acts — a trail cleared, a car freed, a lost child returned — for which no one has taken credit.

Nothing in the record establishes that every report concerns the same individual, or any individual.

On the name. “Bamboo Avenger” first appears in print in a community newsletter in 2004 and is of unknown origin. No report in the collection describes an act of retaliation.Archive note, file 07-A

Equipment Documentation

A.2 · items E-1 to E-6
  1. E-1
    Bamboo staves, a pair. Green, unvarnished, roughly shoulder height, nodes at regular intervals. Carried one in each hand or crossed on the back. Never described as swung or raised.
  2. E-2
    Crossed-stave carry. Staves held in an X by a square fitting on a dark webbing harness. See Artifact D-04.
  3. E-3
    Hooded outerwear. Pale, heavily textured knit; hood up in every account. Front zip, described once as “bright green at the seam.” Small mark on the left chest — see Symbol 001.
  4. E-4
    Gloves. Pale, worn, soiled with earth.
  5. E-5
    Lower garments and footwear. Dark cargo trousers; dark trail shoes or boots.
  6. E-6
    Eyewear. Dark lenses. Reported even at dusk.

Environmental Associations

A.3

The figure is reported almost exclusively where town gives way to woodland: along Ashland Creek, in the upper reaches of Lithia Park, on the watershed trails, and on the mountain. No report places the subject indoors, in a vehicle, or on a paved road other than the mountain road in snow.

Ten of fifteen reports fall between September and November. The archive has not determined whether this reflects the subject or the observers.

Geographic Patterns

All fifteen reports fall inside a band roughly one mile wide that follows Ashland Creek south from the Plaza, through Lithia Park and the watershed, with a separate group on Mount Ashland. None comes from the valley floor east of the interstate or from the hills west of town.

A.5

Recurring Characteristics

  1. R-1
    Stillness. The subject is most often first noticed standing motionless. 9 of 15.
  2. R-2
    Distance. Witnesses rarely report being closer than a stone’s throw. No report describes physical contact, other than the pushed car (RVF-1996-011).
  3. R-3
    Silence. No words are attributed to the subject in any report. Four accounts note the absence of footsteps.
  4. R-4
    Service. A practical task found completed: a path cleared, a bicycle repaired, a wallet returned, a sidewalk shoveled. 11 of 15.
  5. R-5
    Departure unobserved. In 12 of 15 accounts the witness looks away briefly, or the path bends, and the subject is no longer present.
  6. R-6
    Hair. Long, “rope-like” hair below the hood. 6 of 15.

Characteristics are tallied as stated by witnesses and are not adjusted for exaggeration, suggestion, or prior exposure to Reconstruction 001.

About Collection 07

Collection 07 gathers witness statements, field notes, recovered objects and secondary folklore concerning a recurring figure reported in the Rogue Valley of Southern Oregon. The archive records. It does not verify, endorse, or explain.

Evidence remains inconclusive.
