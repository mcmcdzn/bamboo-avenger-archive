F-3.1 · Origin narrative

Where the story begins

Archive status: Unverified Tradition

According to recurring Bamboo Avenger folklore, the figure originated in a hidden bamboo forest known as the Green Vein.

Most versions include:

ElementTellings (of 23)
The Green Vein23
The Whispering Monks19
A fire17
A sole survivor16
A vow of protection14

Tellings disagree on when the fire happened, what caused it, and whether the survivor was a monk, a child in their keeping, or a stranger they took in. The archive does not choose between them.

As told · composite of the most common formF-3.1

Above the town there was a forest that did not belong there: bamboo, green all winter, following the water up into the hills. People called it the Green Vein, and the ones who kept it were called the Whispering Monks, because no one ever heard them raise their voices.

They taught that a thing which bends does not break. They taught listening. They kept the paths open and asked for nothing.

Then there was a fire. Some say lightning, some say it was set. When it was over the forest was gone, and the monks with it — all but one.

The one who walked out carried two stalks of bamboo, scorched at the ends, and made a promise to the hills: to keep the paths, and the people on them, the way the monks had.

Recorded as told. Not evidence.

F-3.2

The Last Survivor

Archive status: Recurring Oral Tradition

One tradition describes the Bamboo Avenger as the final guardian of the Green Vein.

After the destruction of the forest, the survivor emerged carrying two bamboo staves and a single teaching:

Bend. Listen. Act.Recorded in six tellings, 1987 – 2024

The archive notes two echoes and draws no conclusion from either. The circulated copy of Reconstruction 001 carries three words in the same rhythm — Stillness. Awareness. Action. — by an unknown hand. And fragment A-17, a length of bamboo burned at one end, was found on Mount Ashland in 2019.

Reported capabilities

F-3.3 · included as folklore

These are things the stories say the figure can do. None is established fact. Each is listed as told, with its archive status and, where one exists, the nearest parallel in the public record.

C-1 · REPORTED CAPABILITY

Wind Through Reeds

Archive status: Frequently Reported

Stories describe unusual mobility and near-silent movement.

“He doesn’t walk the path. The path moves under him, the way the reeds move when the wind goes through — one after another, never all at once.”

Layer I parallel: four reports note the absence of footsteps (R-3). Twelve describe a departure no one saw (R-5).

C-2 · REPORTED CAPABILITY

Forest Awareness

Archive status: Frequently Reported

Stories describe heightened environmental perception.

“He knows a tree is down before it falls. He knows you are lost before you do.”

Layer I parallel: tasks found completed before anyone reported the need (RVF-2013-022, RVF-2022-012).

C-3 · REPORTED CAPABILITY

Hollow Bamboo

Archive status: Traditional Teaching

A teaching associated with adaptability and resilience.

“Hollow, so it bends. Sealed at the joint, so it does not break.”

Recorded in five tellings. Often said to be the first thing the monks taught.

C-4 · REPORTED CAPABILITY

Forest Memory

Archive status: Unverified Tradition

An ancient tradition stating that bamboo remembers.

“Bamboo remembers. Every joint is a year it lived through. Cut it and it tells you.”

No source for the tradition has been identified. Correspondents date it variously; none offers evidence.

C-5 · REPORTED CAPABILITY

Splinter Strike

Archive status: Folklore

A Whispering Monk martial tradition emphasizing precision over force.

“Strike the splinter, not the tree.”

Layer I note: no report in the collection describes the subject striking anyone. The staves are only ever carried or leaned upon.

Green Vein traditions

Archive status: Unverified Tradition

    The forest grew back underground, along the water, where the fire could not reach. (7 tellings)
  • GV-b
    You can find the entrance only if you are not looking for it. (5)
  • GV-c
    It begins at a bridge, and you only ever see the first part. (4)
  • GV-d
    Where it runs, the ground stays green through the dry months and the birds go quiet. (9)
  • GV-e
    The survivor still keeps it, and keeps it closed. (6)

For the archive’s documentation of the tradition — accounts, diagrams and searches — see Folklore file F-1. No historical evidence has confirmed the existence of such a location.
