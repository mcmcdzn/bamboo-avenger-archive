Folklore file F-1 · Place tradition

The Green Vein Tradition

References to a location known as the Green Vein appear throughout Bamboo Avenger narratives. Descriptions vary considerably. No historical evidence has confirmed the existence of such a location.

Accounts 23Earliest 1987Located NoStatus Folklore
F-1.1

What is said

In most tellings the Green Vein is a route rather than a place: a narrow way through the woods above town that “follows water,” stays green when the hillsides brown in late summer, and is noticeably cooler and quieter than the ground around it. In some tellings the subject is seen entering or leaving it.

Beyond that, accounts diverge. It runs underground, or along the east ridge, or begins at a footbridge and cannot be followed past the first bend. One account places it among stands of bamboo — though no bamboo grove is known in the watershed.

“It follows the water up, and then it doesn’t.”W-0233, 1987 (RVF-1987-003)

Search history

  1. 1998
    Informal walk of the creek corridor by two volunteers following account A. Nothing noted.
  2. 2014
    Desk review of historical maps, ditch and flume records, and early trail inventories. No route or place name corresponds.
  3. 2024
    The junction at E-09 was visited with the marker’s arrow in mind. A short game trail descends to the creek and fades.

A request to readers

The Ashland watershed is a protected municipal water source and much of it is closed to off-trail travel. The archive asks that no one go looking for the Green Vein. Accounts of the tradition, old or new, are welcome in writing.

No historical evidence has confirmed the existence of such a location.

About Collection 07

Collection 07 gathers witness statements, field notes, recovered objects and secondary folklore concerning a recurring figure reported in the Rogue Valley of Southern Oregon. The archive records. It does not verify, endorse, or explain.

Evidence remains inconclusive.
